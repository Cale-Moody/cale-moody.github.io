Boilermaker Special Hammer Stand

As part of Purdue Mechanical Engineering's introduction to manufacturing class, students are taught various skills including milling, turning, laser cutting, and CNC machining. The class is designed for students to gain a fundamental knowledge of manufacturing processes.

The final project of this class was to design a hammer stand and to analyze manufacturability of that stand. While most stands for the class are designed to be decorations, I wanted mine to have mechanical functionality. My lab partners and I came up with the idea of a remote control model of Purdue's mascot, the Boilermaker Special.

As the CAD and Design Lead of the project, I took charge of creating the train model in SolidWorks. The train was designed with several advanced mechanisms, including a rear differential and four-wheel steering. The electronics consist of a Raspberry Pi Zero W for the brains of the project, two servo motors to power the model, a camera hidden in the barrel so the train can be driven in a different room, and a light on the front, similar to the real Boilermaker Special.

The final model features laser-cut wooden body panels, a ladder-frame construction made from milled beams, CNC-cut brackets, 3D printed decorative elements, and custom vinyl decals to replicate the original train.

The Python code runs entirely on the Raspberry Pi. The code hosts a web server for the controls and camera view, which works on any device with a web browser and doesn't require any software download on the client end.

Even with the core functionality achieved, I still want to continue work on this project. Future plans include replacing the Raspberry Pi with an ESP32-S3 with camera module, hiding the battery pack internally, and implementing full two-way communication with a simple buzzer and microphone system.
